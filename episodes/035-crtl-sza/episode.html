Overview

In this episode of Backwards Beats, Dan and Carl dive into CTRL by SZA, ranked #472 on Rolling Stone’s 500 Greatest Albums of All Time. They explore SZA’s groundbreaking debut, her unique blend of R&B, hip hop, and neo-soul, and the bold themes of vulnerability, sexuality, and self-discovery that run through the record. Along the way, they discuss standout tracks, production details, and cultural impact, with plenty of humor, side tangents, and even a campaign to “buy Sesame Street.”

Key Points

  • Album Context: CTRL (2017) is SZA’s debut studio album, following her 2014 EP Z. Released through Top Dawg Entertainment, it features collaborations with Kendrick Lamar, Travis Scott, and more.
  • SZA’s Background: Born Solána Imani Rowe, SZA grew up in New Jersey with supportive but strict parents. Early struggles in college gave way to a music career that quickly gained traction.
  • Artistic Style: SZA is celebrated for her genre-fluid “alt-R&B” style, blending soulful vocals with hip hop cadence, poetic lyricism, and raw emotional honesty.
  • Notable Tracks:
    • Supermodel – stripped-down opener with raw vulnerability.
    • Love Galore (feat. Travis Scott) – explores desire and insecurity.
    • Doves in the Wind (feat. Kendrick Lamar) – satirical and daring commentary on male obsession.
    • Drew Barrymore – inspired by the actress’s authentic persona.
    • The Weekend – a bold anthem about sharing love.
  • Themes: Insecurity, empowerment, sexuality, relationships, and identity all intertwine across the album.
  • Production Notes: Features eclectic instrumentation, detuned textures, and a mix of live and programmed elements that reward listening with quality headphones.
  • Cultural Impact: Four Grammy nominations, over five million U.S. copies sold, and a foundation for her smash sophomore album SOS.
